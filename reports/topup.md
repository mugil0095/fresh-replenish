# A second delivery

One delivery commits the whole day's order before opening. Here the morning carries a share of that order; at the decision hour the store counts what is left and orders up to a target for the rest of the day, delivered 2 hours later (an assumption: the data has no delivery times). The target is the day's calibrated forecast, scaled to each line's usual share of demand still to come and calibrated again on past weeks. Waste and lost sales are charged as in `reports/orders.md`, using LightGBM's forecasts. The decision hour and morning share are chosen on 28 past days; the held-out week only scores them. Intervals come from 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples of series. Generated by `uv run python -m fresh_replenish.decisions.topup`.

## Headline

- **Best plan at r = 9:** order at 13:00 for a 15:00 delivery, with 80% of the one-delivery order sent in the morning. It costs 0.646 (95% CI 0.623 to 0.670) per unit of demand, 32% less than one delivery (0.947; 95% CI of the saving 31% to 33%).

- **Where it comes from:** waste falls from 0.556 to 0.418 per unit of demand, and the fill rate moves from 95.7% to 97.5%. 13% of delivered units come on the top-up.

- **What it leaves out:** the saving does not count what the second delivery itself costs. At r = 9 it is worth 24.3 wasted units per store per day, so a delivery pays only below that.

- **Days with no stockout:** the saving is 23% (95% CI 22% to 24%). Their hourly demand is fully observed, while on other days the afternoon is recovered, not seen. They are also a selected set of days, so the gap is not all down to recovery.

## If stock keeps longer

Everything above throws unsold stock away the same night. The data has no shelf lives, so this lets unsold units keep for up to 3 days at r = 9, oldest sold first. Each policy counts the stock it carries. The week runs twice and the second pass is scored, so the shelf starts with normal stock.

- **Cost-based orders against the most accurate forecast, both at their one-day levels:** 56% at 1 day, 78% at 2 days, 80% at 3 days.

- **Cost-based orders against last week's demand:** 55% at 1 day, 75% at 2 days, 77% at 3 days.

- **Against a fixed multiple of the median, chosen on past weeks:** 6% at 1 day, 11% at 2 days, 8% at 3 days. The multiples are 1.7 at 1 day, 2.2 at 2 days, 2.7 at 3 days. So most of the saving comes from ordering more than the median, and per-line calibrated ranges add the rest.

- **What a second delivery is worth, in wasted units per store per day:** 24.3 at 1 day, 6.4 at 2 days, 2.0 at 3 days.

- **A second delivery against one:** 32% at 1 day, 25% at 2 days, 15% at 3 days. Stock that keeps makes overstock cheaper, so the newsvendor level is no longer the best one: for this comparison, past weeks choose the level of the one delivery again, as well as the top-up's hour and share.
